It has been a busy quarter in the Mautic community following the first [Community Summit](https://www.mautic.org/blog/community/first-mautic-community-summit-a-great-success) back in November.  What follows is a round-up on a team-by-team basis of their activity between January-March 2020.

## Governance

### Team formation

Perhaps the most notable development has been the [community teams](https://contribute.mautic.org/community-structure/governance) forming and starting to meet, with self-elected team leads and assistant team leads taking up the reins.

Teams have moved to asynchronous meetings (an idea we borrowed from the Drupal community!) with meeting notes being [posted onto the forums](https://forum.mautic.org/c/community/43) afterward (note: most recent meetings are not yet shared due to a change in the Slack user interface which has broken our meeting parser!).

### Trello workflows

We have adopted a Trello board-based workflow for each team, being publicly hosted - [www.trello.com/mauticcommunity](http://www.trello.com/mauticcommunity) - with all boards being open and available to anybody. This has meant that we can always see what tasks are outstanding, and have a centralised point for conversations around tasks and projects.  Everyone who wants to get involved in Mautic and contribute is encouraged to check out the Trello boards.

For the purposes of this analysis:

-   Core: those contributing 80% of the activity.
-   Regular: those contributing the next 15% of the activity.
-   Casual: those contributing the last 5% of the activity

#### Issue management efficiency

Image

![Chart showing efficiency at closing issues](https://www.mautic.org/sites/default/files/2020-04/closing-issues-efficiency-mautic-q1-2020.png)

Image caption

Source: [https://dashboard.mautic.org/goto/705e9a7a43597591569e8d72ec4d3843](https://dashboard.mautic.org/goto/705e9a7a43597591569e8d72ec4d3843) BMI

The efficiency at which we process and close issues has been improving this quarter, due to a concerted effort to close outdated issues, merge PR’s and triage more effectively. For reference, Backlog Management Index (BMI), is defined as the number of closed issues divided by the number of open ones in a given period of time. This metric measures our efficiency at closing issues.

## Conclusion

Q1 2020 has been a busy one in the Mautic Community.  A lot of positive steps have been taken to establish a solid foundation for growth.

Teams are becoming more proactive and processes are being established which helps the community to function more effectively.

We have seen, as a result, a significant increase in engagement and have welcomed new contributors to the project as a result.

The next quarter will focus on shipping the Mautic 3 release, and building on these foundations by developing and sharing a formalised roadmap, organising more effectively and further engaging the community.
