I work as a fractional leader - embedded in your team a few days a month, carrying real responsibility for the outcomes rather than handing you a report and leaving. I lead in two closely connected areas: **operations** and **community**.

Since 2020 I have led Mautic, the open source marketing automation project, through corporate acquisition, a spin-out to independence, governance restructuring and financial recovery - work that spans both. I am currently the fractional Director of Operations at Mercury Consortium. That hands-on experience is what I bring to your organisation.

## What a fractional leader gives you

Some organisations need experienced leadership, but not - or not yet - a full-time director in the seat. Perhaps you are between hires, growing faster than your structures can keep up with, or carrying a level of responsibility that has quietly outgrown the person holding it.

A fractional leader gives you that senior experience part-time and ongoing - accountable for real outcomes, integrated into how your team actually works, and at a fraction of the cost and commitment of a full-time appointment. It is not advice from the outside. It is leadership from within, for the days a month you need it.

## Who I work with

- **Open source projects and foundations** navigating the challenges that come with maturity.
- **Startups and scale-ups** that need operational or community leadership before they can justify a full-time hire.
- **Mission-driven organisations, membership bodies and charities** building or running a community, or steadying their operations.

I am particularly drawn to organisations working toward privacy, digital sovereignty, and building genuine alternatives to proprietary tools. If you are trying to prove that a values-led organisation can hold its own against better-funded competition without abandoning its principles, we will probably work well together.

## Operations

Leadership of how an organisation actually runs.

- **Governance and decision-making** - building or refactoring structures that fit your size and stage, and moving from informal leadership to processes that hold, without losing what makes you distinctive.
- **Financial sustainability** - revenue models, funding pathways and membership structures. I have built these from scratch and repaired them when they broke.
- **Strategic planning, restructuring and recovery** - making difficult decisions and steadying operations through change and crisis. I led Mautic through acquisition, spin-out and financial recovery.

## Community

Leadership of the people an organisation depends on.

- **Growth and retention** - attracting contributors or members is one challenge; keeping them engaged and preventing burnout is another. I have worked on both.
- **Health and conflict resolution** - addressing tensions before they turn destructive, and building the kind of governance that handles disagreement well.
- **Marketing and positioning** - helping you articulate your value and reach your audience, with particular strength in privacy- and sovereignty-focused positioning.

### A specialism - open source

Much of my deepest experience is in open source specifically - the particular puzzles of community-built software, foundation governance, and sustaining a project that belongs to everyone and no one. I have written more about that side of the work on my [open source consulting](/about/open-source-consulting/) page.

## How I work

My approach is informed by Buddhist principles around interdependence and skilful means - working with things as they are rather than as we might wish them to be. In practice that means starting from your actual situation rather than an ideal one, considering the whole ecosystem rather than isolated problems, and finding pragmatic solutions that respect your constraints while still moving toward your values.

I do not believe in one-size-fits-all frameworks. What works for an organisation of fifty people will not work for one of five hundred. I start by understanding your specific context, then develop approaches that fit it.

Engagements usually begin with a short piece of discovery to understand where you are, then settle into a regular rhythm - a set number of days a month, shaped around the areas where you need me most. Because this is embedded leadership rather than one-off advice, I work on a minimum term of six months - long enough to understand your context, make real change, and see it hold.
